# D435 on the pan/tilt head: prior-art reconnaissance

Date: 2026-10-27. Persona: prior-art-scout. Scope: a RealSense D435 (no IMU) on the pan/tilt head replacing the ToF +
RGB pair and the gripper camera, on a Raspberry Pi 5 with ROS 2 Jazzy. Four uses: (a) ground plane and plane-relative
obstacle clouds for Nav2, (b) gripper pose in the camera frame from the head camera, (c) calibrating the head camera
against the arm base, (d) images/depth for the LLM agent over MCP.

What already exists in the repo (skimmed, not mapped): `nodes/ground_estimator_cpp` (plane fit + tracker,
plane-relative obstacle/drop bands, IMU-constrained, replay tests), `nodes/head_rgbd_cpp` (ToF depth registered into
the RGB frame), `nodes/head_rgbd_calibration` + `scripts/head_rgbd_calibrate.py` (ChArUco session store, capture,
URDF hand-off, validation), `scripts/solve_camera_mount.py` (mount pose fitted to marker samples, reports rms_px),
`nodes/marker_detector` (QR POIs, uses depth to resolve planar pose ambiguity), `nodes/mcp_server` (annotated camera
images, candidate sets, calibration-sample tools).

Confidence: high = read on the primary source (repo, ROS index, vendor docs); medium = secondary source or
inference from a primary one; low = one forum report or my own reasoning.

## Synthesis

For (c), the most relevant prior art is `robot_calibration`. It is the only maintained, Jazzy-released tool built for
this problem: a pan/tilt head camera watching a target in the gripper, with head joint offsets, mount frames and
intrinsics solved together, and a URDF written out. easy_handeye2 and MoveIt Calibration both assume a camera rigidly
fixed to the base, have no confirmed Jazzy support, and have open accuracy complaints. The SO-101 community evidence is
that plain OpenCV hand-eye with small boards fails on these servo arms. The one working SO-101 + RealSense project
(`lerobot_3d`) moved to markerless URDF-mesh ICP on depth.

For (b), a fiducial on the gripper with `apriltag_ros` or `aruco_ros` (both released for Jazzy), fused with the FK
prior, is the cheap proven route. Depth+FK ICP is the markerless upgrade. DREAM-style learned keypoints are not worth
it here (per-robot training, non-commercial licence).

For (a), the in-tree plane-relative estimator is already better than Nav2's `min_obstacle_height` cut and the
laserscan converters. Intel's Jazzy apt segmenter is the one off-the-shelf thing worth benchmarking it against.

For the Pi 5 itself, no source confirms D435 + Jazzy at 30 fps. Prior reports point at USB 3 negotiation, the RSUSB
backend, alignment cost on the CPU, and WiFi/DDS transport as the failure points, so each one should be measured as an
acceptance criterion.
